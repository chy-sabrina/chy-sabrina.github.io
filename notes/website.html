Why I wanted to build this website

The original idea was quite simple: I wanted to explain my projects in more detail than a CV would allow. I also wanted to share a fuller picture of my work without posting everything publicly on LinkedIn.

As a result, the first version of the site was designed as a single page, which is the version you see at chy-sabrina.github.io. The Notes page was not part of the original plan.

Why I created the Notes page

While writing about my projects, I realized that there were many smaller stories, lessons, and reflections that I wanted to preserve. These notes give me a place to share the process behind the work, not only the final outcome.

I also wanted the Notes page to feel a little more personal and less formal than the main portfolio page. It became a space for thoughts that are more reflective and less polished, while still being meaningful to share.

Behind the Easter Egg

You might notice that this notes page is not completely out in the open. Since these reflections are quite personal, I did not want them to be instantly visible to everyone, but I still wanted to share them with anyone who was curious enough to look a little deeper. That is where the small, security-inspired Easter egg came from.

The Inspiration

The idea came from something I saw during my information security internship. I was reading a penetration testing report and came across a classic authorization issue where a low-privilege user could change a request parameter like user_id to an admin value and gain access to actions they should not have been able to reach.

That little mechanic ended up inspiring this page’s access idea. By default, a visitor is treated as a "guest," and to unlock the notes, they have to switch that identifier to "admin," which is a playful nod to the flaw I saw in practice.

Designing the Clues

At first, I thought about using forced browsing and leaving the page completely unlinked in the navigation bar, so people would have to type the URL manually. But that felt a bit too obscure, since most people would never find it unless they happened to stumble across the GitHub repository.

So I made it a little more playful. I left the link visible in the navigation bar, but without the access code hidden in the source code, this page cannot be opened. It makes it more of a small discovery game for anyone who is curious enough to inspect the page closely.
