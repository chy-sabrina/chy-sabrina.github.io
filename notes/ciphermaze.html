Background

Cipher Maze is the course project for COMP2113. Our team was asked to design and build a text-based mini-game.

Why this theme?

By the time our team was deciding on the theme, COMP1110 had just covered the topic of cybersecurity and cryptography.

In that lecture, we were introduced to Caesar and substitution ciphers, and we also saw how frequency analysis can be used to crack substitution ciphers.

I found that idea especially interesting. A message that looks like meaningless text at first glance can still be deciphered through careful analysis.

That inspired me to propose a cryptography-themed game to my teammates. At the same time, one of them suggested a fog-of-war mechanic, so we combined the two ideas and developed Cipher Maze.

Interesting Things While Working on the Project

At first, we did not plan to include a hint system. During testing, however, I found that I could not reliably solve the numeric and substitution cipher puzzles without assistance. Even with the help of AI, the success rate was still below 50%. That led me to develop a hint system for the medium and hard modes so that the puzzles would remain solvable without external support.

Another issue appeared during testing. In easy mode, the 3x3 map could be completed in just three rooms, so even if a player ignored the puzzles and walked straight to the end, their HP would still remain positive. We discussed this problem and adjusted the damage logic so that players would need to solve at least one puzzle in order to reach the end room.
