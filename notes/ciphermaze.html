Background

Cipher Maze is the course project for COMP2113. We are asked to develop a text-based mini-game.

Why this theme?

By the time our team was deciding the theme of our game, COMP1110 had just gone through the topic "Cybersecurity & Cryptography".

In that lecture, the lecturer introduced Caesar cipher and substitution cipher. He also demonstrated once how to make use of frequency analysis to crack substitution cipher.

I personally find this very interesting and amazing. Just by analyzing the letters' frequency (with a bit of trial-and-error), we can decrypt the text that looks like garbled text at the first glance.

Therefore, I proposed the theme of cryptography game to my teammates. At the same time, one of my teammates proposed to do a Fog of War game. So, we combined the two ideas and developed this game.

Interesting Things when Working on the Project

Originally, we did not plan to implement a hint system. However, when I was testing the game, I totally could not solve the numeric cipher and substitution cipher without the help of AI. And even with the help of AI, the success rate was also lower than 50%. Therefore, I started to develop the hint system for the medium and hard mode (so that I can solve it without using AI).

Also, at first, the HP deducted for each enemy encountered was 25 (total HP: 100). We did not realize anything wrong until the testing stage. For the 3x3 map in easy mode, the shortest path to reach the end only takes 3 rooms. It means that even if the player gave up solving the puzzles and just walk to the end, the final HP will still be positive. We then discussed about this issue and redesigned the HP deduction logic, forcing players to solve at least one puzzle in order to reach the end.
