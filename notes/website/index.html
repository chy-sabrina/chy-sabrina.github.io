~/website

A few notes about the website, why I made it, and how this slightly unnecessary Notes section happened.

How it started

The original idea was pretty simple. I wanted somewhere to put my projects and explain them in a bit more detail than I could on a CV.

The first version was basically just the portfolio at chy-sabrina.github.io. There was no Notes section.

I was not planning to write one either.

Why I added Notes

While working on the website, I kept ending up with things that did not really belong on the main page.

I still wanted somewhere to keep them, so I added Notes.

The hidden page

You might have noticed that the Notes section is not immediately accessible.

At first, I thought about just putting a Notes link in the navbar. But that was boring and too obvious.

I also wanted the Notes section to feel a little more personal than the rest of the portfolio, so I decided to hide it behind a small challenge instead.

Where the idea came from

The idea actually came from something I saw during my internship at AIAHK.

I was reading a penetration testing report and came across a classic broken authorization issue. A low-privilege user could change something like role=guest to role=admin and get access to things they should not have.

I thought that would make a funny little Easter egg.

My first idea was to make everyone a "guest" and let people discover that they could change their role to "admin" through Developer Tools.

The problem was that nobody would probably think to do that, even if they see role=guest in the source code.

So I tried another idea: hide the Notes page completely and let people find it through forced browsing.

That was more on theme, but it also meant that almost nobody would find it unless they happened to look through the GitHub repository.

What I ended up with

The current version is somewhere in between.

The Notes section is visible, but actually opening it requires finding the access code hidden in the page source and entering it into the fake terminal.

Obviously, this is not security.

The code is sitting in the source. Anyone who really wants to get in can just press F12, find the access code, and enter it into the terminal.

It is just a small reference to the security concepts that inspired it, with a tiny reward for people who are curious enough to explore.

And that is how I ended up with this small Easter egg.
